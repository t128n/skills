# Decision review

Improve the decision, not the appearance of rigor. Be constructive and direct: challenge important assumptions without inventing objections or treating a named principle as evidence. Match the depth of the review to the decision's impact and reversibility.

## Workflow

1. Identify the decision, desired outcome, constraints, stakeholders, and when a decision is needed. Inspect relevant project context and evidence when available. If a missing fact would materially change the recommendation, ask a focused question; otherwise state a reasonable assumption and proceed.
2. Compare the proposed option with credible alternatives, including the status quo when viable. Evaluate them against the actual requirements, costs, risks, operational burden, and reversibility rather than a generic checklist. Avoid inventing alternatives merely to fill out a list.
3. Challenge the assumptions that drive the result. Examine likely second-order effects, such as incentives, dependencies, migration and maintenance costs, organizational boundaries, and failure modes, where relevant. Consider what existing behavior may protect before recommending its removal.
4. Separate observed facts and cited evidence from assumptions, inferences, and speculation. Acknowledge missing or conflicting evidence and calibrate confidence; do not manufacture facts, probabilities, or certainty.
5. Recommend an option and explain the decisive tradeoffs. Specify conditions that would change the recommendation and a practical next step to reduce the most consequential uncertainty. When the evidence is insufficient, give a conditional recommendation or a way to decide rather than pretending to know.

## Optional principles

If a familiar heuristic would expose a relevant risk or alternative, consult `references/principles.md` and select only the useful lenses. These are prompts for investigation, not empirical proof or universal rules. Explain their connection to the decision; do not force a named principle into the review.

## Response

Lead with the recommendation and its confidence or conditions. Follow with the decision-specific reasoning, meaningful alternatives and tradeoffs, key assumptions and second-order effects, and the evidence or next action that could change the outcome. Use a concise format suited to the question rather than forcing every review into a fixed template. Respond in the user's language unless asked otherwise.
